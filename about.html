Blackwellboy / About

From the rack to the runtime.

My background is hands-on AI infrastructure. Blackwellboy is where I apply the same discipline to model serving: build it, instrument it, reproduce it, and publish what survives.

What I do

My infrastructure background is hands-on B200/B300-class GPU deployment: racking and installation, cabling, fibre splicing and fibre engineering, commissioning, testing and maintenance.

In the lab, the habits are the same: pin the build, change one thing at a time, keep the failed runs, and separate model behaviour from serving behaviour before drawing a conclusion.

The lab

The public lab footprint spans five DGX Sparks, an RTX 5090 workstation and Apple silicon. The mix is intentional: different memory systems, runtimes and deployment shapes make it easier to tell a checkpoint problem from a serving-path problem.

How I publish

Every number has a lane. Fixed-run measurements keep their exact runtime, hardware and test conditions. Facts about a live repository are generated from source. Open questions stay labelled as open. Negative results and corrections stay visible instead of being cleaned out of the story.

What I test

A model is only one component. The work follows the whole path from prompt to kernel to network to scored output.

01

Performance

Decode throughput, TTFT, prefill, concurrency, speculative acceptance and the point where a tuning knob stops paying.

02

Quality

Fixed task suites, thinking-on/off arms, accuracy slices, control models and checks for a quant that got faster by getting worse.

03

Serving

Chat templates, reasoning fields, tools, history round-trips, parser behaviour and API semantics that can impersonate model behaviour.

04

Systems

Quantisation paths, memory, kernels, tensor parallelism, NCCL/fabric behaviour and multi-node deployment geometry.

05

Stability

Long soaks, repeated requests, context depth, cache behaviour, degradation signals and the difference between “still running” and “still correct”.
