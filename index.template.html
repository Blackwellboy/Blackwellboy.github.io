Available for

What I take on, with links to work that demonstrates each capability.

Independent claim verification

Testing a vendor or community claim under controlled conditions, with the raw data published whichever way it lands.

Shown by the HumanEval+ replication and the identity-prefix study, both of which came out against the claim under test.

Serving and deployment diagnosis

Working out why a model looks broken when the model is fine — often a template, parser, history or serving-stack problem rather than a weights problem.

Shown by multi-turn reasoning-stripping and the registry it fed.

Quantization verification

Taking a third-party quant and establishing whether it holds: whether it builds and serves, what it costs in memory, and whether quality survives against the build you were already running.

Shown by the quant-floor study. The kernel-path half is registry entry 10: the quant label is not the kernel path.

Long-duration production soaks

Running a model inside a real agent pipeline for as long as the question needs, and reporting drift, incidents and integrity behaviour rather than a single throughput number.

Written up: the 12-hour soak.

Multi-node and tensor-parallel deployment

Bringing up models across networked nodes: fabric, topology, serving configuration and the failure modes that only appear once a model spans more than one box.

Shown by Hy3 on dual DGX Spark and MiniMax-M3 on dual DGX Spark.

Eval-harness and measurement auditing

Auditing a benchmark before trusting it: arm-level handicaps, scorer normalisation, contamination screens and harnesses that quietly score a correct answer as wrong.

Shown by the evaluation category of the registry and the HumanEval+ replication, where the original confound was a sampling difference between arms.

What a first pass looks like

Run minefield-doctor against your endpoint with --report and send me the output, along with the number that looks wrong and how you measured it. That creates a useful first artifact before either of us spends an hour guessing.

Contact
