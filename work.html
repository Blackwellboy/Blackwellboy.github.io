Selected work

Six studies. Every figure below is re-derived from the raw data published in the linked directory, and every card states the build and the box it ran on.

Thinking-gate study

Whether this model thinks is decided by the system prompt and the task together, not by the thinking flag. A dense ten-rule persona block dropped firing to 3/40, against 24/40 for a far longer full agent prompt: the gate is non-monotonic in prompt length, which superseded the earlier dose-response reading with a two-axis one. Summarization never fired once in 105 attempts. The same grid on Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B fired 400/400.

Conditions400 grid turns, 10 conditions by 4 task types, n=10 per cell, 450 turns logged including the parser and criteria phases, 0 HTTP errors, single-turn. Laguna S 2.1 NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one DGX Spark GB10. These are within-run n=40 cells on this build and do not generalise across quants or runtimes: at n=492 on llama.cpp with a Q2_K_XL build, apollo-mg measured firing at 445/492 under a comparable apparatus.

HumanEval+ replication

A published claim that thinking-on wins at code generation had been measured with the two arms running at different sampling settings. Re-run with sampling identical on both arms, the gain disappears and the sign reverses: ON 89.84 against OFF 90.85. What does survive is that thinking-on is the steadier arm across seeds, 11 intermittently-passing problems against 17, and that it costs roughly ten times the wall clock, 200.2 s mean per problem against 18.5 s.

Conditions984 requests, 164 problems by 2 arms by 3 seeds, arms interleaved in submission order, identical sampling both arms at temperature 0.7 / top_p 0.95 / top_k 20, max_tokens 12,288, 0 transport errors, evalplus 0.3.1 scoring. NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one GB10.

Multi-turn reasoning-stripping

Standard OpenAI-style clients do not send prior-turn reasoning back, so the chat template renders every earlier turn as an empty think block and the model quietly stops thinking from turn two onward. Identical transcripts fired 10/10 with reasoning preserved and 0/10 stripped, at both depths tested. Several independent testers, myself included, had put this down to the model. The fix is to resend reasoning on assistant history messages.

ConditionsDepth 10 and depth 20 conversations, identical transcripts both arms, max_tokens 4096. Measured on the 0xSero 3.25bpw EXL3 hybrid lane. Confirmation on the NVFP4 production lane is still open and is listed as open in the study.

Identity-prefix study

Testing somebody else's hypothesis, expecting to confirm it. A community wire-level report proposed that long system prompts suppress thinking by evicting the model's trained identity from the front of the prompt. Put to a direct test it fails: moving the identity to the literal first line took firing from 5/40 down to 0/40, the wrong direction. The controls found a real effect at the tail instead, and it is not about identity at all, because any token-matched filler reopens the gate by about the same amount.

Conditionsn=40 per cell, arms interleaved within each run, two builds. NVFP4 bare 2/40 against identity 17/40, neutral filler 10/40, topical filler 11/40. The 3.25bpw hybrid bare 0/40 against 13/40, 14/40, 10/40. Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B null control fired 480/480.

20-cell tuning sweep

Raising speculative depth past K=7 made the model slower, not faster, at every concurrency level tested. The winning cell reached 26.2 tok/s single-stream median and 61.65 tok/s aggregate at concurrency 4. Every losing cell is published.

Conditions20 cells, K 5 to 9 by max-num-seqs 4 to 32, single run per cell. NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one GB10, Hermes bench v1 short subset, TTFT 322 ms at the winner. Not the grid's best aggregate: K=6 with max-num-seqs 8 reached 62.26.

12-hour production soak

3,096 HTTP-200 turns across 409 sessions with zero crashes and zero restarts, inside a real agent pipeline rather than a benchmark loop. Thinking fired on 3 of those turns on a run that requested it every single turn, which is the anomaly the reasoning-stripping study above explains. Twelve hours is the published sample, not a ceiling.

ConditionsOne 12-hour run, production profile K=7 and max-num-seqs=32, max_tokens 8192, thinking requested on every turn. 3,099 rows logged; 3,096 returned HTTP 200. NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one GB10. Carried in TheTom's offlabel guide as external validation. Per-turn log published at lab tip 4507826.

Correction 2026‑07‑25: 409 sessions = 400 completed + 9 killed at the driver token cap; the three extra lines are kind: integrity_probe (all HTTP 200), not a cut mid‑session. Cited at lab tip 4507826.

Public recipes

Two deployments I published as complete bring-up recipes, measured on DGX Spark pairs. Numbers below are copied from their READMEs.

Hy3 — NVFP4, MTP, dual DGX Spark

Serve Tencent Hy3 (295B MoE / 21B active) as an NVFP4 W4A16 build across two DGX Sparks (GB10) — TP=2 over the 200G ConnectX‑7 fabric, native MTP speculative decoding, FP8 KV‑cache, structured hy_v3 tool‑calling, and an OpenAI‑compatible API. One script runs on the head node.

13.1 tok/s single‑stream (no_think, 131K ctx), 9.4 tok/s single‑stream (thinking), 36.9 tok/s 6‑way aggregate; MTP (pos‑1) acceptance 63.5%. Peak/node ~120/121 GiB (84.5 GiB weights + 22 GiB KV + overhead); cold‑start ~14 min. Structured tool calls return finish_reason: tool_calls. Accuracy slice N=100: GSM8K 98.7%, MMLU 90/100 — matches Tencent's published 95.4 / 87.4 within noise.

ConditionsvLLM image eugr/spark-vllm (CUDA 13.0 default toolchain), marlin NVFP4‑MoE path, FP8 KV, hy_v3 parsers patched in‑container. Note: the prebuilt image runs weight‑only FP4 on sm_120 — correct, but ~40% below native‑FP4 speed; from‑source sm_121a is the lever. Full results and flags in that repo's README.

MiniMax‑M3 — stock‑driver, dual DGX Spark

Full 428B MiniMax‑M3 (no pruning) at 34–41 tok/s on two DGX Sparks (GB10) — on the stock DGX Spark OS driver 580.159.03 (CUDA 13.0). W4A16 GPTQ weights + KVarN 4‑bit‑K/2‑bit‑V KV‑cache + EAGLE‑3 speculative decoding, TP=2, all‑Triton attention. No driver swap, no off‑channel packages.

Lane C (standing): 34.0 code / 41.1 structured / 25.5 prose tok/s (acceptance 0.65 / 0.80 / 0.36); deep 17.8 tok/s @113K, TTFT ~147 s; KV pool 217,600 tok. Lane B (fallback, drafterless): ~23 tok/s flat at every shape; prefill ~880 tok/s; KV pool 378,496 tok.

ConditionsRecipes and two small patches in repo; upstream stack pinned at a3refaat/spark-vllm-docker@f942437. Image ghcr.io/blackwellboy/vllm-node-minimax-m3-b12x:cuda13.0-f942437. The stock-driver finding: the b12x attention lane crashes at first token on 13.0 (TMA JIT); KVarN lanes run perfectly on 13.0.

The counts on this site are generated from the registry tree at build time, so they cannot go stale without the build going stale with them. Study figures are not generated: each is a measurement from one run at one revision, and each carries its conditions instead.
