Independent testing and deployment engineering for local AI models.

More than a terabyte of memory across DGX Sparks, RTX 5090s and 3090s, and Apple silicon. Every study below names the box and the build it ran on.

What this is for

Most people do not have the hardware, or the days, to work out why a local model is misbehaving. Whether the fault is the model, the quant, the chat template, or the serving stack usually takes a fleet and a weekend to answer properly.

The work here is doing that crunching once and publishing the answer with the raw data attached, so the next person spends ten minutes instead of three days.

The losing cells, the candidates that failed to reproduce, and the corrections to my own earlier claims are published next to the results that worked. That is what makes the rest of it checkable.

Tools

model-serving-minefield

A registry of serving and config bugs that produce confidently wrong measurements: chat templates, tool parsers, reasoning fields, quantization kernel paths, container toolchains, eval harnesses. 32 entries across 8 categories, each led by the symptom you would actually observe, then the mechanism, the check that catches it, and the fix. A per-model index maps model families to the traps seen on them. Reports from other people are welcome and get credited.

minefield-doctor

One stdlib-only Python file. Point it at your OpenAI-compatible endpoint and it checks that endpoint against the registry. Read-only, no install, no dependencies. Anything it could not check on your stack is reported as not checked, with the reason, rather than guessed.

preflight_template.py

Template forensics for the question every request-shaped check misses: what does the model actually receive at turn N? It renders a marked three-turn conversation through your own serving path and reports whether what you sent survived the round trip. If you only run one check, run this one.

Selected work

  • 20-cell tuning sweep

    Raising speculative depth past K=7 made the model slower, not faster, at every concurrency level tested. The winning cell reached 26.2 tok/s single-stream median and 61.65 tok/s aggregate at concurrency 4. Every losing cell is published.

    20 cells, K 5 to 9 by max-num-seqs 4 to 32. Laguna NVFP4 rev 0761412 on vLLM, one DGX Spark GB10. Hermes bench v1 short subset, single run per cell, TTFT 322 ms at the winner.

  • 12-hour production soak

    3,096 HTTP-200 turns across 409 sessions, zero crashes and zero restarts. Thinking fired on 3 of those turns, on a run that requested thinking every single turn. That anomaly is what the reasoning-stripping study below explains.

    One 12-hour run, production profile K=7 and max-num-seqs=32, max_tokens 8192, thinking requested on every turn. 3,099 turns logged, 3,096 returned HTTP 200. NVFP4 rev 0761412 on vLLM, one GB10. Carried upstream in TheTom's offlabel guide as external validation.

  • Thinking-gate study

    Whether this model thinks is decided by the system prompt and the task together, not by the thinking flag. A dense ten-rule persona block suppressed it hardest at 3/40, harder than a far longer full agent prompt at 24/40, and summarization never fired once in 105 attempts. Reasoning length collapses even where firing does not: median thinking tokens fell from 3,536 bare to 282 under the heaviest agent prompt, which still fired 29/40. The same grid on Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B fired 400/400, so the gate belongs to this model, not to thinking models generally.

    400 grid turns, 10 conditions by 4 task types, n=10 per cell, 450 turns logged including the parser and criteria phases, 0 HTTP errors, single-turn only. NVFP4 rev 0761412 on vLLM, one GB10.

  • HumanEval+ replication

    A published claim that thinking-on wins at code generation had been measured with the two arms running at different sampling settings. Re-run with sampling identical on both arms, the gain disappears and the sign reverses: ON 89.84 against OFF 90.85. What does survive is that thinking-on is the steadier arm across seeds, 11 intermittently-passing problems against 17, and that it costs about ten times the wall clock, 200.2 s mean per problem against 18.5 s.

    984 requests, 164 problems by 2 arms by 3 seeds, arms interleaved in submission order, identical sampling both arms at temperature 0.7 / top_p 0.95 / top_k 20, max_tokens 12,288, 0 transport errors, evalplus 0.3.1 scoring. NVFP4 rev 0761412 on vLLM, one GB10.

  • Multi-turn reasoning-stripping

    Standard OpenAI-style clients do not send prior-turn reasoning back, so the chat template renders every earlier turn as an empty think block and the model quietly stops thinking from turn two onward. Identical transcripts fired 10/10 with reasoning preserved and 0/10 stripped, at both depths tested. Several independent testers, myself included, had put this down to the model. The fix is to resend reasoning on assistant history messages.

    Depth 10 and depth 20 conversations, identical transcripts both arms, max_tokens 4096. Measured on the 0xSero 3.25bpw EXL3 hybrid lane; confirmation on the NVFP4 build is still open.

  • Identity-prefix study

    Testing somebody else's hypothesis rather than my own. A community wire-level report proposed that long system prompts suppress thinking by evicting the model's trained identity from the front of the prompt. Put to a direct test, it fails: moving the identity to the literal first line took firing from 5/40 down to 0/40. The controls turned up a real effect in the other direction, at the tail rather than the head, and it is not about identity at all. Any token-matched tail text reopens the gate by roughly the same amount, on both builds tested.

    n=40 per cell, arms interleaved within each run, two builds. NVFP4 bare 2/40 against identity 17/40, neutral filler 10/40, topical filler 11/40; the 3.25bpw hybrid bare 0/40 against 13/40, 14/40, 10/40. Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B null control fired 480/480.
