About

I work in data centres on B200 and B300 class deployments: cabling and installation, commissioning and testing, then maintenance. Fibre splicing and fibre engineering. Racking the hardware, running and terminating the fibre, bringing it up and keeping it up, at scale.

I run the same class of work on my own cluster, which is why the raw data goes up with the conclusions instead of being described second-hand.

The fleet

A networked cluster of DGX Sparks running tensor-parallel pairs, alongside a multi-GPU workstation stack and Apple silicon, with enough aggregate memory to keep several large models resident at once.

The mix is the instrument rather than the inventory. A fault that reproduces on one architecture and not another is only visible if you have both, and several published findings turn on exactly that. The name of the field a model streams its reasoning into is a property of the serving stack rather than of the model, which took four stacks disagreeing to establish. One entry in the registry is a negative: a cache-isolation flag that behaves as documented on one build and does not on two others, recorded with the same care as a positive, because the conditions are the finding.

Serving stacks

Measured first-hand on llama.cpp, MLX, Ollama, sglang and vLLM. 11 stack pages exist in total; the rest are stacks I have not measured, and each page says so in its own first line. Build and quant formats across those lanes include NVFP4, FP8, GGUF at several quantisation levels, and EXL3-tail builds. Model families include Laguna S 2.1, Qwen 3.5 and 3.6, DeepSeek V4, Gemma, MiniMax and a Mistral-family GGUF. Stacks I have not measured are marked as such in the registry rather than inferred.

Selected work

Six studies. Every figure below is re-derived from the raw data published in the linked directory, and every card states the build and the box it ran on.

Thinking-gate study

Whether this model thinks is decided by the system prompt and the task together, not by the thinking flag. A dense ten-rule persona block dropped firing to 3/40, against 24/40 for a far longer full agent prompt: the gate is non-monotonic in prompt length, which superseded the earlier dose-response reading with a two-axis one. Summarization never fired once in 105 attempts. The same grid on Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B fired 400/400.

Conditions400 grid turns, 10 conditions by 4 task types, n=10 per cell, 450 turns logged including the parser and criteria phases, 0 HTTP errors, single-turn. Laguna S 2.1 NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one DGX Spark GB10. These are within-run n=40 cells on this build and do not generalise across quants or runtimes: at n=492 on llama.cpp with a Q2_K_XL build, apollo-mg measured firing at 445/492 under a comparable apparatus.

HumanEval+ replication

A published claim that thinking-on wins at code generation had been measured with the two arms running at different sampling settings. Re-run with sampling identical on both arms, the gain disappears and the sign reverses: ON 89.84 against OFF 90.85. What does survive is that thinking-on is the steadier arm across seeds, 11 intermittently-passing problems against 17, and that it costs roughly ten times the wall clock, 200.2 s mean per problem against 18.5 s.

Conditions984 requests, 164 problems by 2 arms by 3 seeds, arms interleaved in submission order, identical sampling both arms at temperature 0.7 / top_p 0.95 / top_k 20, max_tokens 12,288, 0 transport errors, evalplus 0.3.1 scoring. NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one GB10.

Multi-turn reasoning-stripping

Standard OpenAI-style clients do not send prior-turn reasoning back, so the chat template renders every earlier turn as an empty think block and the model quietly stops thinking from turn two onward. Identical transcripts fired 10/10 with reasoning preserved and 0/10 stripped, at both depths tested. Several independent testers, myself included, had put this down to the model. The fix is to resend reasoning on assistant history messages.

ConditionsDepth 10 and depth 20 conversations, identical transcripts both arms, max_tokens 4096. Measured on the 0xSero 3.25bpw EXL3 hybrid lane. Confirmation on the NVFP4 production lane is still open and is listed as open in the study.

Identity-prefix study

Testing somebody else's hypothesis, expecting to confirm it. A community wire-level report proposed that long system prompts suppress thinking by evicting the model's trained identity from the front of the prompt. Put to a direct test it fails: moving the identity to the literal first line took firing from 5/40 down to 0/40, the wrong direction. The controls found a real effect at the tail instead, and it is not about identity at all, because any token-matched filler reopens the gate by about the same amount.

Conditionsn=40 per cell, arms interleaved within each run, two builds. NVFP4 bare 2/40 against identity 17/40, neutral filler 10/40, topical filler 11/40. The 3.25bpw hybrid bare 0/40 against 13/40, 14/40, 10/40. Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B null control fired 480/480.

20-cell tuning sweep

Raising speculative depth past K=7 made the model slower, not faster, at every concurrency level tested. The winning cell reached 26.2 tok/s single-stream median and 61.65 tok/s aggregate at concurrency 4. Every losing cell is published.

Conditions20 cells, K 5 to 9 by max-num-seqs 4 to 32, single run per cell. NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one GB10, Hermes bench v1 short subset, TTFT 322 ms at the winner. Not the grid's best aggregate: K=6 with max-num-seqs 8 reached 62.26.

12-hour production soak

3,096 HTTP-200 turns across 409 sessions with zero crashes and zero restarts, inside a real agent pipeline rather than a benchmark loop. Thinking fired on 3 of those turns on a run that requested it every single turn, which is the anomaly the reasoning-stripping study above explains. Twelve hours is the published sample, not a ceiling.

ConditionsOne 12-hour run, production profile K=7 and max-num-seqs=32, max_tokens 8192, thinking requested on every turn. 3,099 rows logged: 3,096 turn records and 3 integrity_probe records (all HTTP 200). 409 sessions in total: 400 completed and 9 killed at the driver's token cap. NVFP4 rev 0761412 under vLLM 0.25.1 on one GB10. Carried in TheTom's offlabel guide as external validation. The per-turn log is published at lab tip 4507826, with free-text previews stripped and structural counters intact. Corrected in place 2026-07-29; the earlier wording miscounted probe rows as incomplete turns and is kept in the lab file for visibility.

What I got wrong

A result you cannot check is worth nothing, and the fastest way to show the checking is real is to show it catching me. This section is not an apology, it is the evidence that the rest of the page is load-bearing.

  • I retracted my own headline finding within days of publishing it. The gate study reported median thinking tokens of 3,536 on a bare prompt against 282 under the heaviest agent prompt, and I read that as prompt dose shortening reasoning. A dedicated 200-turn grid running all five arms interleaved inside one session found depth among fired turns statistically flat, all pairwise p at or above 0.13, with the arm carrying tool schemas holding the highest median. The medians were real measurements; the causal reading was a cross-run artefact. What actually moves depth is task composition and truncation at the tool-call boundary. Retracted in place, with dates, in c7-depth-collapse.
  • I published a refutation of a hypothesis I expected to confirm. The identity-prefix study above was run to verify a community finding, and it came out against it. It shipped with the losing cells intact.
  • I narrowed my own strongest-sounding claim after somebody else's larger sample. "The binary thinking gate is Laguna's own behaviour" is now scoped to the builds I measured, because apollo-mg ran the same model on a different quant and runtime at n=492 and saw no gate closure. His number bounds mine, and it sits inside the card it constrains rather than in a footnote.
  • Losing cells, blocked items and not-testable items are published. All 20 sweep cells including the 19 that lost, and a mining area holding 25 write-ups of candidates that did not reproduce, could not be tested, or are specification only. Several carry confirm and refute criteria written down before the run, so the result cannot be reinterpreted afterwards.
  • Corrections are posted upstream too, including to my own work. When a reader found that I had stated a reasoning field name as a property of the model when it is a property of the serving stack, the correction went into the lab repo, the registry entry and the upstream thread, dated, with the counterexamples named.

Tools

General-purpose, not tied to any one model.

model-serving-minefield

A registry of serving and config bugs that produce confidently wrong measurements. 116 entries across 9 categories: evaluation, memory, quantization, reasoning, routing, runtime, template, tools, versioning. Each entry leads with the symptom you would actually observe, then the mechanism, the check that catches it, and the fix, and each is marked reproduced-here, reported-by-others, or under test.

The Core 12

Reading 116 entries is nobody's plan for the evening. The Core tier is the 12 chosen on evidence of what has actually cost people time: the symptom looks like a property of the model, the check is cheap, and it has already bitten more than one person or more than one stack. Everything else is marked Extended, which means specific rather than lesser.

Playbooks and stack pages

7 ordered checklists for the job you actually arrived with, rather than the symptom you can name: publishing an A/B, thinking dying on turn two, porting a harness to a new server, long context looking broken, reading a soak. Every step names the entry it guards against and the check to run. Alongside them, 11 stack pages covering hf-transformers, llama.cpp, lm-studio, MLX, Ollama, sglang, tabbyapi, tensorrt-llm, text-generation-inference, text-generation-webui and vLLM, each listing the entries that bite hardest there and the three checks to run first.

minefield-doctor

One stdlib-only Python file. Point it at your OpenAI-compatible endpoint and it runs 19 read-only checks in under a minute. Its own README is blunt about the limits: read it as a thinking-stack preflight rather than a doctor, because those checks cluster on reasoning fields, templates, thinking control, tool parsing and ceilings, and say nothing about quantisation kernel paths, container toolchains, memory, MoE routing, harness confounds or long context. A clean run is not a clean bill of health, and anything it could not check is reported as not checked, with the reason.

preflight_template.py

Template forensics for the question every request-shaped check misses: what does the model actually receive at turn N? It renders a marked three-turn conversation through your own serving path and reports whether what you sent survived the round trip. If you only run one check, run this one.

The integrity layer

The registry checks itself, and this page is one of the surfaces it checks. A claim ledger records every retracted or corrected finding together with the distinctive phrasings to search for, and a propagation checker fails the build when any surface still carries a superseded claim without its correction attached. Every check is exercised by a mutation test that reintroduces the exact failure it was built for, and the whole set runs in CI on every push.

Laguna S 2.1 operators guide

A worked example rather than a general tool: the same method run end to end on one model, covering serving config, thinking control, tool format and quantization floor. Every claim links the study directory holding its raw data and every figure carries its conditions.

Independent uptake

Things other people did with this work. Stated narrowly, because every one is checkable.

  • The operators guide was cited as a conformance reference by a project on a stack I have never touched. In youssofal/MTPLX PR 195, the lab is named as the operator guide the checkpoint is documented against, and the guide's own tool-format check did not pass on that MLX stack until a parser dialect bug was fixed. I was not mentioned and made no approach. Credit to davidtai, who also found a scoping error in my own work and reported it.
  • Outside contributors supply entries directly. 12 are credited in the hall of fame for findings or targets they supplied themselves, including a thirteen-entry contribution measured on the contributor's own hardware and a serving target supplied specifically to give the registry a fourth stack. A further 15 people are credited separately as the original reporters of findings mined from public trackers, marked as reported rather than reproduced here.
  • Two pull requests merged upstream, and findings cross-cited both ways. The 12-hour soak is carried in TheTom's offlabel guide as external validation, and the thinking-gate grid is the basis of its rebuilt firing-gate section, replacing an earlier composite drawn from three different stacks.
  • An external contributor's finding confirmed first-hand on different hardware. The multi-turn reasoning-stripping mechanism was independently reported on llama.cpp and reproduced here on a different quant and runtime, which is what separated model behaviour from stack behaviour.

Available for

What I take on. Where a study on this page demonstrates it, it is linked.

Independent claim verification

Testing a vendor or community claim under controlled conditions, with the raw published whichever way it lands.

Shown by the HumanEval+ replication and the identity-prefix study, both of which came out against the claim under test.

Serving and deployment diagnosis

Working out why a model looks broken when the model is fine, which is usually a template, a parser or a history-handling problem rather than a weights problem.

Shown by multi-turn reasoning-stripping and the registry it fed.

Quantization verification

Taking a third-party quant and establishing whether it holds: that it builds and serves, what it costs in memory, and whether quality survives against the build you were already running.

Shown by the quant-floor study, which put a two-tier 3.25bpw expert quant against the NVFP4 build on the same harness. The kernel-path half of this is registry entry 10, the quant label is not the kernel path.

Long-duration production soaks

Running a model inside a real agent pipeline for as long as the question needs, and reporting drift, incidents and integrity behaviour rather than a throughput number.

Written up: the 12-hour soak.

Multi-node and tensor-parallel deployment

Bringing up and running models across networked nodes as tensor-parallel pairs: the fabric underneath, the topology, the serving config on top of it, and the failure modes that only appear once a model spans more than one box.

Eval-harness and measurement auditing

Auditing a benchmark before you trust it: arm-level handicaps, scorer normalisation, contamination screens, and harnesses that quietly score a correct answer as wrong.

Shown by the evaluation category of the registry and by the HumanEval+ replication, where the original confound was a sampling difference between arms.

What a first pass looks like

Run minefield-doctor against your endpoint with --report and send me the output, along with the number that looks wrong and how you measured it. That output is a paste-ready block naming what passed, what failed and what could not be checked, so the first useful artifact exists before either of us has spent an hour on it.
